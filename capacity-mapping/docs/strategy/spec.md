**Problem 

2-3 sentences max. State the core customer pain point with specificity. Include 1-2 direct quotes from customers/users to ground it in reality. Avoid listing multiple problems—pick the most critical one.

  

The San Diego Food Bank delivers food items (refrigerated, frozen and shelf-stable items) to local agencies to distribute to families. Neither the San Diego Food Bank nor the agency knows exactly how much food they can store at a given time. Their space remains underutilized, thereby feeding less families. 

  

Agency - non-profit partners that distribute food to families  

**Vision**

1 sentence. A clear, aspirational statement of what success looks like. Should be memorable and differentiated.

  

The San Diego Food Bank delivers as many complete, balanced boxes as agencies can hold so the most families possible are fed nutritiously. 

**Principles** 

3 bullets max. These are the non-negotiable beliefs that guide tradeoff decisions. Each should imply what you're willing to sacrifice. Format: "X over Y" works well.

  

- Feeding people over filling shelves
    

- Complete nutritional boxes over maximal space allocation 
    
- We want to feed families healthy meals, not give them 10 cans of beans because we have it 
    

- Capacity measured today over capacity on the floor plan
    

- Inventory is tightly controlled and so we can rely on that data; use AI for a second opinion

Output Metric: number of people fed 

  

Input Metrics

**Solution** 

3-4 key initiatives max. Each should directly address the problem and align with your principles. Use subheadings. Include enough detail to be concrete but not so much that it becomes a spec.

  

- Initial Data Collection
    

- Assumption: San Diego Food Bank and partner agencies currently have an accurate mapping of inventory (e.g. what food, where is it) up until it is delivered to the family  
    
- A representative from the agency maps capacity at physical location including refrigerated, frozen and shelf-stable storage 
    
- This gives the San Diego Food Bank clarity on total capacity, regardless of actual utilization today
    

- Food bank decision maker that has x number of people to feed and can distribute efficiently to the agencies they partner with based on capacity
    

- 100,000 people need food
    
- Food bank knows each person need 3 bags of rice, 2 cans of beans, frozen peas (let’s call this a box because you cannot have these things separated across locations)
    
- Now you know the number of “boxes”
    
- Now you know the space capacity for each location BASED ON CURRENT INVENTORY (not initial, important) 
    
- How many boxes should go to each agency
